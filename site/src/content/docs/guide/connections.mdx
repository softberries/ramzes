A connection is one place messages can go: a Slack channel, a mailbox, a webhook URL. The Connections
page lists them with their health, and holds the catalogue you add new ones from. This page covers the
screen; what to enter for each service is in the [channel pages](/ramzes/channels/overview/).

<Screenshot name="connections" caption="Four connections with their deliveries of the last 24 hours, and below them the catalogue of channels a new connection can be made from." maxHeight="32rem" />

## The list

| Column | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | The connection's name. Under it, in red, "Last error:" with the most recent error of the last 24 hours, and the result of a test message if you sent one. |
| **Kind** | The channel it uses, for example Webhook or Slack app. |
| **Delivered, 24 h** | Deliveries that arrived in the last 24 hours, with a small bar showing the share that arrived of those that ended. |
| **Failed, 24 h** | Deliveries that failed in the last 24 hours. "N waiting" underneath counts the ones still pending. |
| **State** | **Active**, or **Paused until** a time; see below. |
| **Changed** | How long ago the connection was last saved. |
| **Actions** | **Send test message**, **Setup** (Slack app connections only), **Edit** and **Delete**. |

The search box looks in the name, the kind and the last error. Headings sort the list; sorting by
**State** puts paused connections first.

## Adding a connection

<Steps>

1. Under **Add a connection**, pick a channel. The catalogue is grouped into **Chat**, **Paging**,
   **Email and push**, **Generic** and, once you have written one, **Your own**. A card says what the
   channel does and, after "Can do:", whether it supports buttons, threads, or updating its messages.

2. Fill in the form. **Name** starts as the channel's name; change it to something that says where
   messages go, such as `#ops (Slack)`. The other fields come from the channel, each with a line of
   help underneath.

3. Press **Add connection**. The server validates the fields; a problem is shown above the buttons.

4. Press **Send test message** on the new row to check it.

</Steps>

For what each field means, go to the channel's page: [Slack](/ramzes/channels/slack/),
[Microsoft Teams](/ramzes/channels/teams/), [Discord](/ramzes/channels/discord/),
[Email](/ramzes/channels/email/), [ntfy, Telegram and Pushover](/ramzes/channels/push/),
[PagerDuty and Opsgenie](/ramzes/channels/paging/) and [Webhook](/ramzes/channels/webhook/).

A connection sends nothing until a route targets it. Add it to a route under
[Routes](/ramzes/guide/routes/).

### Slack app connections

After you save a Slack app connection, a card titled "One more step in Slack for" and the connection's
name shows the address Slack must send button presses to, with a **Copy** button. **Setup** on the
connection's row shows the same card again later. The steps on the Slack side are in
[Slack](/ramzes/channels/slack/).

## Testing a connection

**Send test message** sends a real message through the connection, to its own destination, with the
title "Test message from ramzes" and the body "If you can read this, the connection works."

Under the connection's name the row then shows "Test message delivered.", or "Test failed:" with what
the service answered. A test is not an event: it does not appear in the feed, in the counts or in
Failures.

## Paused connections

When deliveries to a connection keep failing, ramzes pauses it and holds its deliveries back instead of
sending them into a service that is down. The **State** column then reads, for example, **Paused until
14:32** "after 5 failed attempts in a row". The pause ends by itself.

**Resume now** ends the pause at once and sends what was waiting. Use it when you know the service is
back, or after fixing the connection. When and for how long a connection is paused is described in
[Delivery and retries](/ramzes/concepts/delivery/).

Paused connections are also listed under **Failing connections** on the
[Overview](/ramzes/guide/overview/).

## Editing

**Edit** opens the connection's form with its current settings. Secret fields are empty: stored secrets
are never sent back to the browser.

- Leave every secret field empty to keep the stored secrets.
- To replace a secret, type the new value and press **Save connection**. On a connection with more
  than one secret, such as a Slack app with its bot token and signing secret, the ones you leave empty
  are kept.

## Deleting

**Delete** asks first and lists the routes that send to the connection, at once or in a step of their
escalation chain. Those routes are not
changed: they fail to deliver to it until you edit them. With no such route the dialog says "No route
sends to it."

## Your own connectors

The **Your own connectors** card at the bottom is for a service ramzes ships no channel for. You
describe the HTTP request to send as YAML, and the connector then appears in the catalogue under
**Your own**, tagged "your own".

- **Write a connector** opens an editor with a small working example to start from. **Add connector**
  saves it.
- The table lists your definitions by **Name**, **Kind** and **Added**, with **Edit** and **Delete**.
- A connector that a connection still uses cannot be deleted. The server names the connections:
  "this connector is used by: … Delete those connections first."

The format is documented in [Your own connector](/ramzes/channels/custom/) and
[Connector definitions](/ramzes/reference/connector-definitions/).
