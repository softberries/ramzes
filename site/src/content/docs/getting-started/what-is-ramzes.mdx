ramzes is a self-hosted notification router for internal and operational events. Your services send
events to it over HTTP. It stores every event, matches it against routes, delivers it to the channels
those routes name, and shows in a web UI what happened to each one and why.

It is one binary with an embedded web UI. It keeps its data in a SQLite file next to it, or in
Postgres if you point it at one.

<Aside type="caution" title="Status">
All four milestones of the design are built and covered by automated tests. Nothing has been
released, and no connector has been run against the live service it talks to. See
[Verified claims](/ramzes/verified/) for what was checked and how.
</Aside>

## The problem

In a system of several services, each one that needs to tell a person something ends up with its own
Slack webhook call, its own SMTP settings and its own idea of when to stay quiet. That leaves you
with:

- credentials for chat and email spread over many services;
- no one place that shows what was sent, to whom, and whether it arrived;
- no way to quiet a noisy sender without deploying it again;
- nothing that notices a message went unanswered and tells someone else.

With ramzes a service only says what happened: a topic, a title, a severity, some labels. Who is
told, on which channel, how often and in what words is configuration in ramzes, changed in one place
and recorded when it changes.

## What it is not

- **Not a product-notification platform.** It is for messages to the people who run a system. It
  has no end-user accounts, subscriber preferences, unsubscribe links or per-recipient inboxes.
- **Not a message broker.** There is no publish/subscribe and no consumer protocol. Events come in
  over HTTP and leave as messages to channels; services do not read events back from ramzes.
- **Not an on-call scheduler.** An escalation chain tells other connections after a delay. There
  are no rotas, shifts or calendars; a route names connections, not people on duty. For paging,
  ramzes can send to PagerDuty or Opsgenie.
- **Not highly available.** It runs as one instance and does not coordinate several.

## Who it is for

Teams that run their own services and want operational messages (failed jobs, build results,
alerts, payment errors) to go through one place they control. Everyone who can sign in is an
administrator, so it fits a team that trusts its members with the whole configuration. See
[Limits and what is not built](/ramzes/limits/).

## The parts

**Sources.** A source is a sender with its own key. Your services post events in ramzes's format to
`/v1/events`. Alertmanager, Grafana, GitHub, GitLab and Sentry post their own webhook formats to a
per-source address, and a source of the kind "Any JSON" carries a mapping you write. See
[The events API](/ramzes/sending/events-api/) and [Sources](/ramzes/guide/sources/).

**Events.** An event has a topic, a title, a severity, a status (`firing` or `resolved`), an
optional body, labels, a payload of any JSON and an optional dedup key. Every accepted event is
stored before the sender gets its answer. See [Events](/ramzes/concepts/events/).

**Routes.** Routes are an ordered list. Each one matches events by topic pattern, minimum severity,
source, labels and an optional expression, and names the connections to send to. A route can stop
the ones below it from being evaluated. See [Routes](/ramzes/concepts/routes/).

**Notifications.** When a route matches, ramzes opens a notification for each of the route's
targets. A notification collects the events that belong together, and it is what gets sent,
acknowledged and resolved. See [Notifications](/ramzes/concepts/notifications/).

**Noise control.** A route can count duplicates instead of sending them again, collect events into
one message, hold messages during quiet hours and limit how many go out in a window. Silences
suppress matching events on every route for a period. See
[Noise control](/ramzes/concepts/noise-control/).

**Escalation and acknowledgement.** A route can tell someone else when nobody acknowledges within a
delay. People acknowledge from the UI, the API, a link in the message, or a button in Slack. See
[Escalation and acknowledgement](/ramzes/concepts/escalation/).

**Connections.** A connection is one configured channel: a Slack webhook or app, a Teams or Discord
webhook, an SMTP account, ntfy, Telegram, Pushover, PagerDuty, Opsgenie, a webhook to any URL, or a
connector you define in YAML. Deliveries are retried, and a connection that keeps failing is
paused. See [Channels](/ramzes/channels/overview/) and
[Delivery and retries](/ramzes/concepts/delivery/).

**Templates.** A template words the title and body of a message for one route target. Without one,
the event's own title and body are sent. See [Templates](/ramzes/concepts/templates/).

**The web UI.** The feed lists every event with what became of it, and each event has a page that
shows the routes it matched and every delivery attempt with the service's answer. The same UI holds
the configuration and its history. See [Tour of the app](/ramzes/guide/tour/).
